## The tools

One operator cannot baby-sit a Vault cluster, guard a fragile `tfstate`, and
pray each night that Consul avoids a split-brain. Each tool here takes one of
those burdens and settles it for good. Each does one job, stands on its own,
and attaches to assets you already own. Compose them the way you chain `grep`
and `awk`, or use one and ignore the rest.

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="Kuberina" icon="seti:kubernetes">
		**Offline scheduling for heterogeneous Kubernetes.** Reformulates pod
		placement as multi-dimensional bin packing, borrowed from how mega-vessel
		shipping lines stow containers. Produces a reviewable blueprint instead of
		an opaque runtime decision.

		[Overview](/kuberina/) · [Tutorial](/kuberina/tutorials/first-blueprint/) · [Paper](/research/kuberina-stowage-scheduling/)
	</Card>
	<Card title="Helvilette" icon="seti:config">
		**Pull-based GitOps delivery for Ansible.** Agents pull playbooks from Git
		and reconcile at the systemd layer. One last SSH session to install it,
		then port 22 closes forever.

		[Overview](/helvilette/) · [Tutorial](/helvilette/tutorials/quickstart/)
	</Card>
	<Card title="Kallisto" icon="seti:lock">
		**A read-only secrets resolver on localhost.** It speaks the Vault KV-v2
		API over one encrypted file on an S3-compatible bucket. Your request path
		keeps working while Vault is sealed, failing over, or mid-upgrade, and
		while the bucket is down. Adopting it is one environment variable. So is
		removing it.

		[Overview](/kallisto/) · [Tutorial](/kallisto/tutorials/quickstart/)
	</Card>
</CardGrid>

## Why this exists

The capabilities that keep infrastructure calm, such as secret rotation,
reconciliation loops and reviewed placement, usually arrive bundled inside an
enterprise suite, a paid tier, or a platform team you do not have. Small
organisations patch around the gap with duct tape that looks fine from outside
and rots from within, and learn to leave running things untouched.

Naughtian brings those capabilities to a single operator. The approach can be
blunt and the pace deliberate. The goal is to end the pain permanently, with no
extra payment asked to make it stop.

## What every tool promises

- **You can read all of it.** The codebase is small enough to audit in a day.
  An operator needs no certification to run it, and an ordinary AI agent can
  follow it.
- **It holds no state worth fearing.** At rest the software is inert, so backup
  and restore are yours to do at will. It leans on distributed consensus as
  little as it can, because the most resilient consensus protocol is none.
- **It hides nothing and decides nothing for you.** Every action is observable,
  and the final call stays in human hands.
- **It takes no hostages.** Remove any tool and your playbooks, manifests and
  Vault-speaking applications keep working. Your assets come back to you.

Vault manages your secrets, and someone still has to manage Vault. Anything
that needs a quorum to keep running cannot be the bottom of the stack, because
losing a vote stops everything built on it.

## Where each one sits

Kuberina decides *where workloads go* before anything runs. Helvilette keeps
*the machines underneath* converged on their desired state. Kallisto serves
*the secrets those workloads need* from the same machine, without a round trip
to the root of trust. When any of them fails, the layers around it keep
working.

## Your pain, your answer

These tools are one answer to pain that someone faced directly. It may not fit
yours. The source is open for that reason: bring the operational stories these
projects cannot yet resolve, fork a repository and reshape it, or build what is
missing.

> I have recognized my pain. Where is my solution? If nobody built it yet, I
> will create it myself.

**Kalena**, **Kaeliir** and **Ginnungagap** are the next answers in progress,
and will appear here as they become real. Their names come from the same saga
as the rest. See [names and mythology](/ecosystem/naming/).
